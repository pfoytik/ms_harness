SUMMARY. The follow-up sweep produced critical scenarios whose exit weights are only moderate, but this report cannot yet say whether they contradict the SME's expectation. Scenarios 468 (exits 0.51 / 0.55, resilience 25.1%) and 340 (0.52 / 0.49, 24.1%) are critical without either exit node being strongly compromised. In both, observed resilience sits almost exactly on the exit-only floor of 100 x (1 - w33) x (1 - w34) (22.1% and 24.4%; the small shortfall for 340 is within Monte Carlo noise), which means upstream nodes almost always gave the attacker a foothold at an exit. Which upstream segments supplied that foothold is the SME question, and the five scenarios shown do not separate it. Mid-ladder rung 10-13 averages 0.40 and 0.54 in these two scenarios, and the near-exit rung 23-32 averages 0.54 and 0.49, so I cannot honestly label them as confirming or contradicting the SME on this evidence. The other three top scenarios (73, 15, 110) each carry one exit at or above 0.84 and are CONSISTENT WITH the SME expectation.

THE SURPRISE SCORING WORKS AGAINST THIS QUESTION. The element potentials rank nodes 10 (0.068) and 12 (0.062) as the most important non-exit nodes. As a 4-node rung that every path must cross, 10-13 is a structural chokepoint. Under paths_to_exit, elevating them raises a scenario's potential and lowers its surprise score, so the surprise score treats a mid-ladder-driven critical scenario as "expected", even though it would contradict the SME's stated view that segments closer to the end nodes matter most. The topological potential and the SME's text disagree about which nodes are the expected drivers, and the analysis is currently following the topology.

THE PRIM BOX. This box is the weakest so far: 30 scenarios with 20% precision, well below the 45% critical share of the pooled data (268 of 600), and a bias ratio of 0 with completeness 0.398. Most bounds only trim the edges of a range. The exit bounds (33 in [0.20, 0.60], 34 >= 0.36) mainly reflect where the follow-up sampled. The box should not be read as a mechanism.

MOST ACTIONABLE INSIGHT. To test the SME claim directly, the next sweep should remove the near-exit segments as a route to success and see whether critical outcomes persist. If they do, those critical scenarios contradict the SME and point to the mid-ladder chokepoint. For future campaigns, the SME expectation can be made the basis of the surprise score (element_potential "sme" with the near-exit nodes as expected drivers), so PRIM searches for exactly these contradicting scenarios instead of penalizing them.

RECOMMENDATION. Run a targeted sweep with exits 33 and 34 in [0.45, 0.60] as before, the near-exit rung (nodes 23-32) held low at or below 0.30, and all other nodes free. Critical scenarios that appear under these constraints are, by construction, critical without strong compromise of the segments closest to the end nodes. Their number, and their dependence on nodes 10-13, would be the test of the SME expectation.
