SUMMARY. None of the top five critical scenarios contradicts the SME's expectation; all five are CONSISTENT WITH it. Each has one exit node, the segment closest to the end of the ladder, at a very high compromise probability: scenario 73 (node 34 = 0.91, resilience 15.0%), 165 (34 = 0.96, 6.0%), 50 (33 = 0.96, 3.0%), 47 (33 = 0.90, 14.2%) and 119 (34 = 0.94, 9.3%). They rank as "surprising" only because many other nodes are below nominal, which lowers their paths_to_exit scenario potential. The analysis has not yet found a critical scenario in which the end-of-ladder segments are uncompromised.

WHY THAT IS EXPECTED. Under the stated input semantics an exit node can never be compromised with probability above its own weight, so resilience can never fall below 100 x (1 - w33) x (1 - w34). For a scenario to be critical (resilience <= 25.5%), that floor must be below 25.5%, which requires at least one exit weight well above ~0.5. So with this metric and threshold, critical outcomes always involve a strongly compromised exit; the SME's expectation about the very last segment is close to guaranteed by the model's structure rather than discovered. The more interesting question is what happens upstream: observed resilience in the top scenarios is consistently higher than that exit-only floor (73: 15.0% vs 4.4%; 47: 14.2% vs 7.2%; 119: 9.3% vs 4.3%), because an exit can only be reached when one of its parents is compromised. How much upstream nodes close that gap is where findings that contradict the SME could still appear: for example, if mid-ladder nodes such as 10 and 12 (the highest element potentials apart from the exits, in a 4-node rung that every path must cross) matter more than the rung immediately before the exits (23-32).

THE PRIM BOX. The box is not yet a useful region. It stopped at the minimum support of 30 scenarios, its precision (43.3%) is below the 50% base rate of critical scenarios, and most constraints only trim the outer few percent of a range. The one substantive bound is node 34 >= 0.237, again pointing at the exits. The low bias ratio (0.037) says the few critical scenarios in the box come from the low-potential quintiles, as the surprise score intends; given the precision, though, that reflects the box being drawn around low-potential scenarios rather than a concentrated surprising mechanism.

MOST ACTIONABLE INSIGHT. Because exit weights alone set a floor on resilience, an unconstrained sweep spends most of its variation on the exits and hides upstream effects. To test the SME's claim that segments closer to the end nodes drive critical outcomes, the exits should be held in a band where criticality is possible but not guaranteed, and the upstream structure should be allowed to decide.

RECOMMENDATION. Run a targeted sweep with both exit nodes 33 and 34 held between 0.45 and 0.60, where the exit-only floor is about 16-30%, straddling the 25.5% threshold, and leave every other node free. Within that band, compare the influence of the rung immediately before the exits (nodes 23-32) with the mid-ladder rung (nodes 10-13). If the near-exit rung dominates, the SME expectation holds. If mid-ladder nodes dominate, those critical scenarios contradict it and are the surprising findings to pursue.
